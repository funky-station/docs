## **\#Overview**

This doc provides some craftable items requiring crew coordination, primarily for use against command and security.

## **\#Background**

With the further focus on crew vs command/aegis conflict, I think a major problem lies in the usefulness imbalance within the crew's department. In such conflicts, it often becomes the case that all weapon production falls into the hands of logistics and science, both of whom mostly work separately.

In addition to the reasons stated by Teasq, I think that our new antag-optional design lacks one critical aspect: progression. The current weapon systems mostly lock combat to the few roles that already get weapons roundstart, while sec has both a two-tiered weapon system and the ability to print out additional weapon upgrades like the laser cannon. This means that the optimal play for a revolt is mainly to gather as many people as possible and simply rush at sec with what you already have. There is little need for subterfuge or actual substantial cooperation.

Cross-department contraband

## **\#Game Design Rationale**

Since Funky aims to have growing tensions and resentment between security and crew, I think the crew should have some ability to covertly and substantially increase their power if left too long unobserved.

Additionally, I think it significantly increases the amount of variety you will see round-to-round, giving the crew useful new tools to play with ranging from serious to completely silly

## **\#Roundflow & Player interaction**

These items will realistically not be utilized every round, when they are used, it will likely be later in the round due to needing coordination of resources. 

I think the most interesting impacts of these types of cross-department items will actually be how they are treated before being used, there are a bunch of situations that would not otherwise come up that I think would be fun for both Aeghis and the rest of the crew. 

Smuggling: smuggling specific items from department to department allows for some fantastic roleplay, needing stealth and coordination, additionally, it creates some fun gray zones for officers when it comes to contraband. Should you arrest somebody for carrying tools capable of being made into a deadly weapon? What missing items do you report to the commandant or not? Is the doctor getting a defibrillator into science to craft a weapon or just to save someone? 

Hidden bases and stashes: when the crew has more items to collect, that means they have more things to hide, you can’t feasibly carry the bulky weapons around all the time, and crafting them in the heat of the moment is implausible. Meaning that having these weapons be significantly stronger than most you get access to by default encourages careful hiding of them.

Gives crew resources to lose: similar to the last note, these items are really good, meaning losing them hurts, a lot. I think this will likely encourage not just rushing sec before they can get an idea of the growing unrest, but instead waiting, keeping everything hidden until the crew is armed enough to stand up against their oppressors. Additionally, some of the items. like the scrip-printer and CAT give extra ways of covertly sabotaging the higher-ups without having to do a full-blown riot

Lastly, from a purely logistics-oriented perspective, it makes for some really fun bounty items, ones that require you to gather small pieces to slap together for a huge reward.
