## Overview

The purpose of this document is to give crew more tools for violent revolt, following the officialization of
the ***[Antagonist-Optional Design](https://docs.funkystation.org/design-proposals/antag-optional-design.html)***
document. It establishes three categories of weaponry: improvised, makeshift and chance.

Improvised weaponry are unconventional weapons that may not necessarily be designed for violence, but 
have a greater capacity for it than simply your hands. This includes, but is not limited to:
broken bottles, chairs, stools, tools such as crowbars and wrenches, toolboxes, etc.

Makeshift weaponry are weapons that are explicitly designed for violence, but require some form of production
to acquire. This includes but is not limited to:
shanks, spears, improvised bow & quiver, improvised pistol, improvised shotgun, etc.

Chance weaponry are weapons that can be found in maintenance/by chance, but are otherwise not produced or capable
of being made elsewhere by crew. In short, it's maintenance loot. This includes but is not limited to:
machetes, katanas, disposable one-use guns, syndicate knuckle dusters, etc.

The goal of this document is two-fold: to expand the options crew at large have to violently revolt, mug, fight
or defend themselves with and to make combat overall feel more deadly and authentic.
This document **does not** seek to put them on equal terms in terms of one-on-one combat capability. The crew's
strength will always lie in their numbers and guerilla tactics.

## Background

Previously, under antag-oriented design crew's capacity for violence and revolt is kept closely under check as
to not disrupt security too much and allow antagonists to run rampant. This design alienated crew from the core
loop of the game, with people oftentimes feeling "cheated" when they did not roll antagonist. 

Now, Funky is moving closer and closer to antag-optional design and instead focusing on the conflict that
can be found between command and security versus the crew at large. This document seeks to expand the ways in
which crew can fight back, as crew's current capacity for violent revolt is somewhat insufficient to stand against
security's superior coordination and arms.

## Features to be added

### Repair system

This is a needed system for items to be repairable. Currently, the repair system only allows for repairs via 
welding. This needs to be expanded to also allow repairing via materials. An example: repairing the makeshift
vest using durathread, or repairing a shield using steel sheets.

### Improvised weapons

Improvised weapons will be seeing sweeping changes rather than additions. In other words, quality of life and 
authenticity changes. This includes:

1. Most if not all improvised weapons will be able to be thrown, ending their trajectory when they hit another
    entity and doing the corresponding thrown damage. This is to give people more options and making throwing
    objects more satisfying. For instance: you throw a crowbar at someone, it will fly towards them and
    damage them with a resounding "Thwunk" as it bounces off of their head. Sharper objects will embed instead.

2. Tools/mundane objects that make sense will have their damage bumped up. This is both for authenticity's sake and
    to incentivize their use to begin with. For instance: the broken bottle, which normally does 5 piercing damage
    will have its damage increased to 12. This makes taking the time to break a bottle on the floor and using the
    shard to fight someone in the bar play out a little more authentically. 

### Makeshift weapons

1. Autolathe safety wire inventory. This is one of the document's core features. All autolathes now have a safety
    wire, similar to vending machines and doors, that can be snipped. This removes the autolathe's safety
    limiters and allows you to print things from an illegal inventory. However, it also makes the lathe
    incredibly loud while lathing and occasionally notifies everyone around of the breach of warranty.
    While this inventory may expand during the implementation of this document, the current inventory includes:
    steel balls, gun receiver (moved from the standard inventory), makeshift shield (moved from construction menu),
    , makeshift vest, and steel blade. 

2. Gunpowder/ammunition production. This is one of the document's core features. The aim of these changes is to make 
    gunpowder, while still a process, simpler and more accessible to produce. Gunpowder is now craftable with a 
    chemical mixture of: space cleaner + potassium + salt + ash (exact numbers TBD). This means 
    ammunition can now be produced with a bit of ingenuity, hard work, teamwork or simply theft. 
    makeshift ammunition will also be overhauled, with shotgun shells not being the one and only option. 
    Most ammunition will be made using filed steel rods, gunpowder and plastic. The current shotgun rounds
    will still be available, but primarily as "flechette" rounds, as it fires glass shards at someone with the intent
    of making them bleed - a lot.

3. Makeshift vest. This is made from cloth + durathread. It will provide similar protection values as a standard
    armor vest, but acts similarly to the makeshift shield. It will keep deteriorating the more it takes damage, and 
    eventually break if not repaired using durathread.

4. Slingshot + steel balls. Crafted using steel + LV wire and can shoot singular small objects. This requires the item to be loaded,
    the slingshot to be wielded, then aimed and shot. It is very similar in function to the bow, but its conventional
    ammunition are steel balls. It also allows for more creative applications, such as firing bottles.

5. Laser musket. Crafted using a modular receiver, rifle stock, 2 rods, 2 machine parts, 3 glass and 3 cloth. 
    The musket is loaded with batteries, with higher capacity batteries allowing for more shots before running out.
    The musket packs a decent punch with hitscan lasers, but it costs a lot of charge and is therefore not very high
    capacity. Taking a battery out and putting a new one in both require very long do-afters.

6. Tripwires. Crafted using rods + any wire. Tripwires are directional similar to directional windows, and running
    over a tripwire causes one to trip over it and the wire to snap. You can attach bombs, such as modular grenades,
    pipe bombs or firebombs to trigger when the tripwire is triggered. Tripwires are not triggered when walked over.
    Nos-lips do not prevent tripping. The wires can also be broken by damaging them.

7. Molotov cocktails. These are molotovs, simple. Crafted from any alcoholic bottle, some cloth and an igniter. Throwing it
    causes it to break, spill its liquid and ignite.

8Makeshift bladed weaponry. Crafted using the makeshift blade + cloth + steel/plastic. Recipe will depend on the item crafted.
    This includes: makeshift dagger, makeshift throwing knives/stars, makeshift spear, makeshift machete.

8. Miscellaneous damage/damage profile improvements. Improvements to the damage profile of shivs and spears so they
    are actually worth using over a chair or crowbar. Improvements to the damage of other craftable weapons, such as
    the bladed flatcap and baseball bat to be consistent with the overall damage increases.

### Chance weapons

These will largely receive the same changes as improvised weapons. There are no planned additions... except!

Disposable guns. This will be a rare addition to maintenance loot. A discontinued line of small, low caliber pistols with a 
built-in magazine that cannot be reloaded. You have to use a wirecutter to cut off the tag, which releases the safety and voids
the warranty on the pistol. (spoiler: the warranty expired about 34 years ago.)

## Game design rationale

### Antagonist-optional design

With Funky moving away from antagonist-oriented design, crew can finally be allowed to have access to weapons that have 
impact. These changes are a necessary foundation for crew to grow into a formidable threat to security if not quashed,
with the threat of security not maintaining its iron grip being all-out revolt or war.

### Maintaining authenticity

Many of the changes suggested are an attempt to make combat play out more authentically and intuitively. Breaking a bottle
over a table and using it *should* be rewarded and *should* hurt. This principle is applied to each respective object in turn,
with chairs becoming a possible blunt weapon, crowbars being more violent, kitchen knives being very dangerous to throw, etc.

### Dynamic environment

While Funky may not maintain the motto of "Anything should be possible" anymore, the proposed changes opens up a gateway for things
that *feel* like they should be possible, while simultaneously giving crew more agency in how they approach threats physically.
This applies a more conventional physical threat behind each crew's grumbling, complaints and makes "Viva!" feel like an
actionable threat, rather than an empty one in most cases.

## Considerations

### Administration - Powergaming

These items may need to be looked out for and powergaming situations may be a little more nuanced overall, as someone
could be making a weapon on green alert for things such as revenge, out of desperation for scrip, food, etc. While this document doesn't
directly engage with scrip, it does allow for more violent/accessible means of committing crime than crew options currently offer.
